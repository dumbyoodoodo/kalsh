## 1. Executive summary

**Research question.** Are Kalshi NYC daily-high (`KXHIGHNY`) market prices,
observed at six pre-registered fixed times-to-close (72h, 48h, 24h, 12h,
6h, 2h) and grouped into ten pre-registered probability deciles,
calibrated within transaction costs — i.e., does realized YES-settlement
frequency track each cell's mean implied probability closely enough that
the deviation never exceeds that cell's own round-trip cost (fee +
half-spread), with statistical confidence, and does that hold up in a
temporally later, non-overlapping replication slice?

**Motivation.** H0005 is this project's efficient-market gate: the null
hypothesis every future edge claim must clear first. An "edge" against a
well-calibrated market would be a bug in the model claiming it, not a real
opportunity; H0005 exists to establish whether — and precisely *where*, if
anywhere — Kalshi's daily-temperature markets deviate from calibration by
more than it costs to trade against the deviation.

**Methodology.** A pre-registered, frozen protocol (mid-price primary,
day-clustered percentile bootstrap, Bonferroni-adjusted discovery screen,
temporally-later hold-out replication screen, minimum-N-per-cell power
gate, ask/bid executable-price robustness disclosure) executed exactly
once against a pinned dataset, with a required immediate byte-identical
re-run before any interpretation.

**Canonical outcome: Inconclusive (power-limited).** No primary-family
cell cleared both the Bonferroni-adjusted discovery screen and the
hold-out replication screen. Six cells cleared the discovery screen alone
but were underpowered (fewer than the pre-registered 20 hold-out days) to
independently confirm a replicated finding — the exact scenario PREREG
Sec 10's minimum-N gate exists to catch. This is the second, corrected
execution against the same pinned dataset; the first execution (superseded)
returned a spurious "Rejected" caused by two implementation defects
discovered and corrected between the two runs (Section 4).

## 3. Scientific findings

**What was learned.**

- The pinned archive (`exp-20260721-h0005`, 402 distinct `KXHIGHNY` markets,
  66 valid settlement days, 2026-05-15 → 2026-07-20) clears the
  pre-registered 60-day floor and split cleanly into a 33-day discovery
  slice and a 32-day hold-out slice (1-day embargo).
- Secondary calibration diagnostics (Brier score, log loss, ECE,
  calibration slope/intercept — computed per horizon, independent of the
  cost-band comparison and therefore unaffected by either implementation
  correction) show prices at every horizon with data beat the
  always-base-rate baseline, and error shrinks monotonically as the
  horizon shortens (Brier 0.099 at 24h → 0.0000465 at 2h) — consistent
  with, but not itself a test of, prices converging toward the
  eventually-realized outcome as settlement approaches.
- 402 daily-high markets concentrate heavily in the extreme deciles
  (0-10% and, at shorter horizons, 90-100%) rather than the middle of the
  probability range — a data-shape fact, not a calibration finding, but
  one that materially determined which cells had enough statistical power
  to be informative at all (see below).

**What was *not* learned.**

- **Whether Kalshi NYC daily-high prices are calibrated within costs.**
  The pre-registered decision rule did not resolve to Confirmed or
  Rejected. No inference about market efficiency at this station/series
  can be drawn from this run.
- **Anything about the 72h or 48h horizons.** Zero `KXHIGHNY` markets have
  any candle within the 15-minute staleness tolerance of `close − 72h` or
  `close − 48h` in this pinned archive — all 20 of those horizons' cells
  are empty by construction (`N=0`, `calibration_error: null`, per
  AMENDMENT Finding 7). This directly contradicts PREREG Sec 6's stated
  justification for including 72h ("the candle archive's own backfill
  window... comfortably covers it for every market"); empirically, in
  this pinned archive, it does not. The effective primary family for this
  run was 40 cells (4 horizons × 10 deciles), not the nominal 60.
- **Anything about most of the remaining 40 cells.** 53 of 60 nominal
  cells fell below the 20-day hold-out minimum-N gate.

**Why the result is INCONCLUSIVE.** Per AMENDMENT Finding 4's evaluation
order: step 1 (total-day floor, 66 ≥ 60) passed; step 2 (any cell clears
both screens) found none; step 3 (any cell cleared discovery alone but was
underpowered in hold-out) found six such cells, so the run terminates
**inconclusive-for-power** rather than reaching step 4 (Confirmed). Only 7
of 60 nominal cells had ≥20 hold-out days at all — (2h, decile 0), (2h,
decile 9), (6h, decile 0), (6h, decile 9), (12h, decile 0), (24h, decile
0), (24h, decile 3) — and none of those seven cleared the discovery
screen. The six cells that *did* clear discovery are exactly the sparsest
ones in the grid.

**Underpowered cells (cleared discovery, insufficient hold-out N):**

| Horizon | Decile | Discovery days | Hold-out days | Discovery calibration error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24h | 6 | 2 | 3 | −0.328 |
| 24h | 8 | 5 | 1 | −0.138 |
| 12h | 3 | 8 | 7 | +0.356 |
| 12h | 8 | 2 | 4 | −0.180 |
| 6h | 1 | 1 | 1 | +0.105 |
| 6h | 8 | 1 | 2 | −0.115 |

**Ambiguous evidence.** These six cells' point estimates are large in
magnitude (10-35 percentage points) — large enough, relative to a small
cost band, to clear a strict Bonferroni-adjusted discovery screen — but
each rests on 1-8 discovery days and 1-7 hold-out days. This is precisely
the low-N regime in which a handful of unusual days can produce a
striking point estimate that a properly-powered slice can neither confirm
nor refute; PREREG Sec 10 pre-registered exactly this failure mode
("cells below this threshold are reported with their point estimates and
explicitly marked `insufficient_n`... and can never independently trigger
'rejected' regardless of their point estimate"). No interpretation beyond
that pre-registered treatment is offered here — per this task's
instruction, this record does not speculate beyond the preregistered
analysis.

## 4. Engineering lessons

Four distinct implementation issues were found across this hypothesis's
lifecycle. All four are documented here regardless of when they were
found or by whom, so the record is complete.

### 4.1 Cost-band unit mismatch

- **Root cause.** `calibration_error` (and its bootstrap CI) was computed
  as a fractional `[0,1]` probability deviation
  (`mid_price_cents/100 - outcome`), while `cost_band_cents` was computed
  and stored in raw cents (`fee_cents + half_spread_cents`, always ≥ 1
  since `contract_fee_cents` never returns zero within its domain). The
  discovery/hold-out screens compared these two incommensurable
  quantities directly (`lower_abs > cost_band_cents`), which — since a
  fractional deviation is bounded to `[0,1]` and the cents-scale band is
  always `≥ 1` — is unsatisfiable for *any* input. "Rejected" was
  mathematically unreachable.
- **Why earlier review missed it.** The implementation matched
  AMENDMENT Finding 5's exact fee-invocation wording literally; the unit
  mismatch was in the *comparison*, not the formula each side individually
  implements correctly. A read-through against the frozen text checks
  each formula against its own specification; it does not automatically
  check that two independently-correct formulas are expressed on a common
  scale before being compared to each other.
- **How it was corrected.** `cost_band_cents` converted to the same
  `[0,1]` scale at the point of comparison (`/100`) — the identical
  conversion the frozen protocol already uses at PREREG Sec 8
  (`ask_implied = yes_ask_close_cents/100`). `calibration_error`'s own
  units, the fee formula, and every threshold were unchanged.
- **Regression tests added.** Boundary-equality (no pass on an exact tie
  under the *original*, pre-`Fraction` comparison), just-inside/
  just-outside the cost band, and an end-to-end `build_cells` →
  `evaluate_outcome` test proving "Rejected" is reachable at all post-fix.
- **Preventative measure adopted.** Every numeric comparison between two
  independently-derived quantities should have an explicit unit-
  reconciliation test exercising both directions (fails-to-clear and
  clears) — not just a formula-matches-the-spec test for each side in
  isolation. This class of defect is invisible to a code review that
  checks each formula against its own citation.

### 4.2 Fee-formula domain boundary (100¢ ask prices)

- **Root cause.** PREREG Sec 7 asserts "Kalshi's own price ceiling is
  99¢," so the top decile bucket ([90,100], closed both ends) "naturally
  lands inside it." Empirically, in the pinned archive,
  `yes_ask_close_cents` reaches 100 in 13,682/761,475 (1.8%) of
  executable candle rows. `round_half_up(mid_price_cents)` can therefore
  equal 100, outside `contract_fee_cents`'s documented 1-99¢ domain — a
  guard that function enforces by design (`h0007_fees.py`, frozen/
  verified for H0007), raising `ValueError` rather than silently
  computing a wrong value there.
- **Why earlier review missed it.** This is a **factual assumption in the
  frozen preregistration** that happened to be wrong for this archive —
  no code review of the implementation against the protocol text could
  have caught it, because the implementation faithfully encoded exactly
  what the protocol assumed. It was only discoverable against real data,
  which is precisely why it surfaced as a crash during execution rather
  than during QA.
- **How it was corrected.** The fee is evaluated at its own well-defined
  mathematical limit: `ceil(multiplier·P·(1−P)·contracts) = 0` at
  `P = 0` or `P = 1`, since one factor is zero. This is the same frozen
  formula evaluated at a domain edge `contract_fee_cents` defensively
  declines to compute itself — not a new fee model, and `contract_fee_cents`
  itself was not modified. The alternative (excluding these
  observations) was rejected as a *worse* deviation: it would have
  silently biased the sample away from exactly the extreme decile H0005's
  own anchoring/favorite-longshot economic motivation is most interested
  in, violating PREREG Sec 5's survivorship guard.
- **Regression tests added.** Fee at the 100¢ and 0¢ boundaries evaluates
  to exactly 0 (not a crash, not a clamp to a different price);
  `contract_fee_cents` behavior across the full 1-99¢ domain is unchanged.
- **Preventative measure adopted.** A preregistration's stated factual
  assumptions about the data (not its statistical *choices*) should be
  spot-checked against the real archive as part of the pre-execution
  checklist, not assumed correct because they read as plausible. This is
  now an explicit erratum recorded against PREREG Sec 7 in the
  implementation note, without altering the frozen document itself.

### 4.3 Half-spread rounding

- **Root cause.** PREREG Sec 9 defines `half_spread_cents = (yes_ask_close_cents
  − yes_bid_close_cents) / 2` as a real-valued quantity; AMENDMENT
  Finding 5 froze the **fee term's** rounding (`round_half_up`) but never
  specified rounding the half-spread. The first execution's
  implementation nonetheless applied Python's built-in `round()` —
  banker's rounding (round-half-to-even), a mode this platform's own
  established convention explicitly rejects ("every rounding rule already
  established on this platform rounds up, never to nearest-even or
  down") — which silently zeroed a 1-cent spread's cost contribution
  (`round(0.5) == 0`).
- **Why earlier review missed it.** This defect passed the original
  pre-registration code review, the implementation QA pass, the
  template-parity polish pass, and even the author's own cost-band
  unit-mismatch review (Section 4.1) — including tests that were written
  to assert the rounded behavior as correct. It surfaced only when a
  dedicated execution-integrity review re-derived the sole clearing
  cell's arithmetic directly from the frozen protocol text, rather than
  from what the code happened to compute, and found the two didn't
  match.
- **How it was corrected.** `cost_band_cents()` now computes the
  half-spread as an exact `Fraction(yes_ask_close_cents -
  yes_bid_close_cents, 2)`, never rounded, and returns an exact `Fraction`
  rather than an `int`. Averaging a cell's per-observation cost bands
  (`statistics.mean` over `Fraction` values) is itself exact. Values are
  converted to `float` only at JSON-serialization time, strictly after
  every screen decision has already been made.
- **Regression tests added.** A 1-cent spread yields exactly 0.5¢ (not
  0¢); a 3-cent spread yields exactly 1.5¢ (not 2¢, the other direction
  banker's rounding can err); cell-level averaging of `Fraction`-valued
  bands is exact; and an explicit regression test reconstructing the
  superseded `fee + round(half_spread)` formula shows it zeroes the
  1-cent-spread case that decided the first execution's clearing cell.
- **Preventative measure adopted.** Any frozen formula containing a
  division is now treated as needing an explicit "is this rounded, and by
  which rule?" check against the frozen text before implementation —
  silence in the protocol is not license to pick a rounding mode, and
  this platform's own established convention (rounds up, never to
  nearest-even) should be the default assumption checked against, not
  a language's built-in default.

### 4.4 Floating-point comparison at an exact tie

- **Root cause.** The discovery/hold-out screen comparison
  (`|CI lower bound| > cost_band`) was decided in raw IEEE-754 binary
  float arithmetic. At the pinned dataset's sole clearing cell, every
  discovery-slice observation shared an identical deviation
  (mid=99.5¢, outcome=1 always), so the bootstrap CI collapsed to an
  exact point, `abs(99.5/100 - 1)` — which is *mathematically* exactly
  `1/200`, but whose IEEE-754 representation carries roughly `4×10⁻¹⁸`
  of error above the true value. Once Section 4.3's fix made the
  corrected cost band *also* exactly `1/200`, the comparison became an
  exact tie decided entirely by which side's floating-point
  representation happened to round up — a decision made by binary
  representation, not by the data. This directly contravenes CLAUDE.md's
  non-negotiable rule to use `Decimal`/exact arithmetic for money and
  price-derived quantities, not binary float.
- **Why earlier review missed it.** This defect was masked by, and only
  became visible after, fixing Section 4.3 — before that fix, the
  (incorrectly rounded) cost band was 0, not `1/200`, so no tie existed
  to notice. It is also a genuinely rare failure mode (requires every
  observation in a cell to share an identical value), which is why it
  surfaced only at the one degenerate cell in the entire 60-cell grid
  where it happened to be decision-relevant.
- **How it was corrected.** A new `_exceeds_cost_band()` function decides
  the comparison by converting both operands to `Decimal`, quantized to a
  fixed `1e-9` precision, before the strict `>` — far finer than any
  genuine difference cents-scale, day-averaged data can produce, so it
  strips representation noise (`~1e-16`–`1e-18`) without ever suppressing
  a real signal. The bootstrap itself (`day_clustered_bootstrap_ci`,
  `_percentile`) was explicitly left untouched, per the remediation's own
  scope — only the final comparison changed.
- **Regression tests added.** An exact tie evaluates `False`; a genuine
  excess still evaluates `True` (the fix must not make the screen
  unsatisfiable, the opposite failure mode from Section 4.1); a genuine
  shortfall evaluates `False`; the Decimal-quantization helper is tested
  directly to confirm it strips float noise without erasing a real
  difference; and a regression test reconstructs the superseded raw-float
  comparison at the exact tie that decided the first execution's verdict,
  showing it evaluates `True` purely from representation noise.
- **Preventative measure adopted.** Any comparison whose operands are
  derived through independent floating-point computation paths (here: a
  bootstrap-derived empirical value vs. a formula-derived exact value)
  should be treated as a potential exact-tie site and decided in
  controlled-precision or exact arithmetic by default, not only after a
  tie is observed to cause a problem. More broadly: **a "does this match
  the frozen text" review should re-derive at least one concrete,
  decision-relevant number from the text by hand (or by an independent
  script) and compare it to the code's output** — reading code
  side-by-side with prose catches wording mismatches; it does not catch
  arithmetic that both "looks right" and is subtly wrong.

## 5. Research-process lessons

- **Preregistration review should include an empirical spot-check of
  stated data assumptions**, not just of statistical choices. Section
  4.2's 99¢-ceiling assumption was internally consistent and plausible on
  its face; only a query against the real archive revealed it was false
  for this dataset. A cheap, standard step — "does the pinned dataset's
  raw column ranges match every quantitative claim the preregistration
  makes about the data" — would have caught this before execution.
- **Implementation review should include re-deriving at least one
  concrete number by hand from the frozen text**, independent of the
  code, for whichever cell/case is decision-critical once real data is in
  hand. Both Section 4.3 and 4.4 passed multiple rounds of code-vs-text
  comparison and were only caught when the integrity review computed the
  clearing cell's arithmetic itself, from the text, and compared the
  result to what the code produced — a fundamentally different (and more
  expensive, but higher-yield) check than re-reading the code.
- **Execution review needs a distinct, narrowly-scoped mandate from
  interpretation.** The execution-integrity review in this hypothesis's
  history was explicitly instructed not to reinterpret the protocol,
  critique the hypothesis, or analyze calibration — only to evaluate
  execution validity. That narrow scope is what made it effective: it is
  a different kind of check than a scientific read of the result, and
  conflating the two risks either skipping the arithmetic re-derivation
  (because the result "looks reasonable") or drowning it in unrelated
  scientific commentary.
- **Integrity review should be triggered whenever a run produces a
  decision that turns on a small number of observations or an unusually
  clean number** (an exact 0, an exact tie, a cost band that rounds to
  zero) — these are exactly the situations where a boundary-condition
  defect is both more likely to exist and more likely to be
  decision-relevant, as this hypothesis's own clearing cell (29-observation,
  single-value discovery slice) demonstrates.
- **Deterministic verification (immediate byte-identical re-run) should
  remain mandatory and automatic**, including after any implementation
  correction, not only for the "first" execution. Both executions in this
  hypothesis's history were re-run and verified byte-identical before any
  result was reported — this did not catch either defect (both defects
  are deterministic, so they reproduce identically), but it remains a
  necessary, cheap check for a different class of bug (non-determinism),
  and should not be skipped merely because a run has already been
  corrected once.
- **A "canonical vs. superseded" artifact convention should be the
  default, not an ad hoc response.** Renaming (not deleting) the first
  execution's results with a `-SUPERSEDED` suffix and a prominent
  in-file notice, while writing a dedicated implementation-note document
  explaining exactly what changed and why, produced a complete,
  navigable audit trail without requiring any git history to exist yet.
  This pattern should be the standard response to any invalidated
  execution going forward, not something improvised each time.

## 6. Reusable components

Infrastructure validated or exercised by H0005 that future hypotheses can
build on directly, with no further verification needed for the properties
already established:

- **Dataset generation** (`kalshi-weather dataset build --which
  market_price_weather`) — produces a versioned, content-hashed,
  manifest-tracked Parquet export; used identically for H0007 and H0005.
- **Settlement reconstruction** (`settlement/labels.py`'s `implied_result`,
  `RECONSTRUCTION_VERSION`) — reused verbatim for outcome derivation, not
  reimplemented.
- **Point-in-time alignment** (`dataset/asof.py`'s `asof_latest`) — the
  as-of candle selection engine, reused verbatim; 0 leakage violations
  previously verified over the full 761,475-row archive (ADR 0008), and
  exercised again here with a 15-minute staleness tolerance.
- **Bootstrap framework** (`day_clustered_bootstrap_ci`, `_percentile`) —
  a day-clustered percentile bootstrap generalized from H0007's own
  pattern to accept arbitrary CI percentiles, letting one function serve
  both the Bonferroni-adjusted discovery screen and the standard-95%
  hold-out screen. Left untouched by both implementation corrections;
  available as-is for any future hypothesis needing a day-clustered CI.
- **Fee calculations** (`scripts/h0007_fees.py`'s `contract_fee_cents`,
  `KALSHI_WEATHER_TAKER_FEE_CONFIG`) — reused verbatim across H0007 and
  H0005 without modification. Its documented 1-99¢ domain restriction is
  now a known, worked-around edge case for any future hypothesis pricing
  near the extremes: evaluate the same formula at its own P=0/P=1 limit
  (fee=0) rather than excluding or clamping such observations.
- **Execution framework** (the `run()` orchestration pattern, `argparse`
  CLI, dataset-dir/results-JSON/markdown-report triad) — the same shape
  used by H0007, now also proven at H0005's larger, more heterogeneous
  cell grid.
- **Manifest generation and validation** (`validate_manifest`, the
  `TEMPLATE-*-manifest.json` schema-parity discipline) — a reusable
  pattern (required-key-path walking, `ManifestValidationError` on any
  gap) for ensuring a results JSON is schema-complete before it is ever
  written to disk; directly reusable for any future hypothesis with its
  own manifest template.
- **Deterministic execution discipline** (single seeded `random.Random`
  instance, documented consumption order, mandatory immediate re-run with
  byte-identical verification) — proven twice in H0005's own history
  (before and after the implementation correction) and once before that
  in H0007; should remain the standard for every future execution.
- **Audit workflow** (pre-registration → institutional audit → amendment
  → implementation → QA → execution → execution-integrity review →
  correction → canonical rerun → superseded-artifact retention) — the
  complete, now twice-exercised (H0007, H0005) process this project uses
  to go from a frozen protocol to a trustworthy result. This post-mortem
  is itself part of that workflow's standard final step.
- **Exact-arithmetic comparison pattern** (`Fraction` for exact rational
  computation through aggregation, `Decimal` at a controlled quantization
  precision for the final comparison against a float-derived quantity) —
  newly established by this hypothesis's remediation; directly reusable
  wherever a future hypothesis needs to compare a bootstrap-derived
  (necessarily float) quantity against a formula-derived (exactly
  computable) threshold.

## 7. Recommendation on revisiting H0005

**H0005 should be revisited, but only when specific, identifiable
conditions change — it is not permanently closed, and it is not left
open-ended either.**

Two concrete, actionable gaps drove the inconclusive result, and both are
data-accumulation questions, not design flaws:

1. **72h/48h horizon coverage.** The pinned archive has zero candles
   within tolerance of these two horizons for any `KXHIGHNY` market.
   Extending the candle backfill window further before each market's
   close (or beginning collection earlier relative to `close_time`) would
   populate these cells in a future re-run. This is a data-platform
   question (candle archive depth), out of scope for a re-run of this
   experiment itself.
2. **Sparse mid-probability deciles.** 53 of 60 nominal cells — most of
   them the middle deciles — fell below the 20-day hold-out minimum. More
   accumulated settlement days, simply by increasing the pool from which
   each cell's day-count draws, would raise several of the 6 discovery-
   clearing-but-underpowered cells to a decidable state without changing
   anything about the design.
