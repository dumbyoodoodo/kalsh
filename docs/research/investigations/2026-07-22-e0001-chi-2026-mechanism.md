# E0001 — Exploratory mechanism analysis: the CHI 2026 tmax revision elevation

Date: 2026-07-22. **Status: EXPLORATORY.** This study contains no
hypothesis test, no decision rule, no confidence-interval-based claim,
and no p-values. Every number below is a descriptive count or fraction,
reported to rank candidate mechanisms and seed future pre-registered
H-series studies. Generated by
`scripts/e0001_chi_mechanism_exploration.py` (read-only) against the
pinned `exp-20260722-h0013-replication` frame (hash verified in the
facts file) plus the raw archived CLI product text in
`raw_api_payloads`; full numbers in `2026-07-22-e0001-facts.json`;
figures in `figures/e0001/`. All windows below are season-matched
(Jan 1 – Jul 21) unless stated.

**Question.** H0014 found a decision-level rise in CHI's 2026 tmax
revision rate (25.3% vs 15.6% season-matched history) with no
registered operational co-occurrence and no corroboration at NYC. What
plausibly caused it?

**Headline (exploratory):** the raw CLI products carry the
time-of-occurrence of each day's maximum — the field H0011 said would
be needed for mechanism work — and they show that **CHI's daily maximum
occurred at or after the 4 PM preliminary cutoff far more often in
2026 (38.2% of days) than in any prior year (24.6–29.6%)**. A
post-cutoff maximum mechanically forces an upward preliminary→final
revision, and essentially all of the revision elevation sits in that
channel. The proximate mechanism looks meteorological (diurnal-timing
composition), not operational.

## 1. Observed facts

Numbered facts only; interpretation deferred to §3.

**F1 — Revisions are overwhelmingly upward, everywhere, always.** CHI
2026: 50/50 revisions upward. CHI 2023–25: 90/93 upward. NYC all years:
122/122 upward. Magnitude mix in CHI 2026 (68% ≤1°F, 18% >2°F) is
similar to history (57–72% ≤1°F).

**F2 — The preliminary product's cutoff and timing are unchanged.**
Every parsed preliminary at both stations, all four years, says
"AS OF 0400 PM LOCAL TIME"; first-issuance local time is ~16:35 (median
16.57–16.63h) in every year at both stations.

**F3 — Corrections are rare and flat.** Products with a corrected WMO
header (CCA/CCB): CHI 2/2/3/1 by year; NYC 0/0/1/1. No 2026 anomaly.

**F4 — The daily maximum occurred late (≥16:00 local) much more often
at CHI in 2026.** By year: 24.6%, 29.4%, 29.6%, **38.2%**. Evening-deep
maxima (≥18:00): 7.5%, 7.6%, 6.0%, **12.0%** — roughly double. (fig1,
fig2)

**F5 — NYC drifted the same direction, but less.** Late-max fraction
25.1%, 25.0%, 31.5%, 34.4%; evening-deep flat (9.7/11.0/10.2/8.9%). Its
revision rate drifted correspondingly (14.7% → 17.1%), below H0014's
decision threshold. (fig2)

**F6 — Revision risk is concentrated in the late-max stratum at both
stations in every year.** P(revised | late max) ranges 34–53% across
station-years; P(revised | early max) ranges 2.2–6.8%. CHI 2026:
39/73 = 53% late vs 8/118 = 6.8% early — the highest late-conversion in
the table, but the same qualitative structure as everywhere else.
(fig3)

**F7 — The CHI monthly pattern of late maxima matches the monthly
pattern of the revision elevation.** Late-max share, hist → 2026: Jan
29→50%, Mar 29→48%, Apr 32→43%, Jun 24→40% (May 22→19%, Jul 33→29%
flat/down) — the same winter/spring months where H0014's monthly
diagnostic showed the revision elevation. (fig4)

**F8 — General weather context at CHI 2026:** mean final tmax normal
(60.0°F vs 59.0–62.2 prior years); *fewer* hot days (≥85°F: 21 vs
28–29); day-over-day absolute tmax change elevated (7.9°F vs 6.7–6.8).
NYC 2026 shows no such volatility elevation (6.4 vs 5.6–6.8).

**F9 — tmin-side stability.** Evening-occurring minima counts are flat
at both stations (CHI 58–68/year), consistent with H0014's finding that
tmin behavior did not move.

**F10 — Product-format stability, with a small shared parsing residue.**
The occurrence-time line parsed for ≥96% of final products in every
station-year; unparsed counts were 0–2/year through 2025 and 7 at
*both* stations in 2026 (~3.5%) — a shared, small format/missing-data
residue worth a look during any productionization, not a CHI-specific
change. CHI and NYC use different time renderings ("2:59 PM" vs
"357 PM"), stable across years.

## 3. Candidate mechanisms — evidence for and against

**M1. Diurnal-timing composition: more days whose maximum occurs after
the 4 PM preliminary cutoff (weather-driven).**
*For:* F4 (the late-max jump is the dominant moved quantity); F6 (the
revision channel runs through the late-max stratum in all 8
station-years, so a late-max rise must raise revisions); F7 (monthly
coincidence); F1 (all-upward revisions are exactly what post-cutoff
maxima produce); F5 (NYC moved the same direction proportionally to its
smaller late-max drift — one mechanism, two magnitudes); F8
(day-over-day volatility elevated at CHI only, consistent with more
frontal/advective days shifting maxima off the early-afternoon solar
peak).
*Against:* nothing found. The residual not explained by composition
alone is the conversion rise (M2).

**M2. Higher conversion within late-max days (later/larger post-cutoff
gains).**
*For:* CHI 2026 P(revised | late) = 53% vs 39–49% prior; evening-deep
share doubled (F4) — later maxima have more time to exceed the 4 PM
provisional value.
*Against:* conversion fluctuates historically (NYC 2023 also 53%); this
is a secondary modifier of M1, not an independent mechanism.

**M3. Operational/reporting change (product wording, correction
practice, issuance timing, collection pipeline).**
*For:* nothing. *Against:* F2 (cutoff and timing constant), F3
(corrections flat), F10 (format stable), plus H0014's registered
indicators (cadence 2.020 vs 2.008; completeness ≥98%; single-issuance
flat). Every operational axis inspected is quiet.

**M4. Extreme-heat frequency.**
*For:* nothing. *Against:* F8 — 2026 CHI had *fewer* ≥85°F days and a
normal mean; the elevation concentrates in winter/spring (F7), not the
warm season.

**M5. Seasonal composition.**
Already controlled by H0014's season-matched windows; F7 shows the
signal lives *within* the matched window. Residual seasonal effects are
a caveat, not a mechanism.

**M6. Archive/collection artifact (missing days, backfill differences,
duplicate handling).**
*For:* nothing. *Against:* completeness ≥98% every window (H0014);
the pinned frame's hash re-verified here; F10's parsing residue is
shared across stations and tiny.

## 4. Plausibility ranking

1. **M1 — diurnal-timing composition (weather-driven late maxima):**
   strongly supported on five independent facts; explains the
   CHI-vs-NYC difference as one mechanism at two magnitudes.
2. **M2 — elevated late-day conversion:** plausible secondary
   amplifier; partially just "M1, deeper."
3. **M5 — residual seasonality:** minor caveat at most.
4. **M3 — operational change:** effectively refuted on every inspected
   axis.
5. **M4 — extreme heat:** refuted (direction of evidence is opposite).
6. **M6 — archive artifact:** refuted for the mechanisms inspected.

**Explicit over-interpretation guard:** these are descriptive
decompositions of one partial year at one station. "Strongly supported"
means *consistent with every fact inspected*, not confirmed. Whether
the 2026 late-max jump is itself a fluctuation (one unusual winter/
spring) or a persistent shift is precisely what no exploratory analysis
can decide — and the counts involved (73 late-max days) are small.
Nothing here reopens or modifies H0011/H0013/H0014's frozen outcomes.

## 5. Recommendations for future H-series confirmatory studies

1. **H0015 (proposed now, entry added to HYPOTHESES.md): the
   late-maximum channel explains the revision-rate structure.**
   Pre-register on data not used here (full-2026 windows when
   available, plus DEN/LAX as new stations): (a) P(revised | early max)
   is small and stable; (b) station-year revision-rate differences are
   driven by the late-max fraction (predicted revision rate from the
   composition identity tracks the observed rate); (c) at CHI/NYC,
   conditional conversion rates are year-stable once occurrence depth
   is controlled. This converts E0001's decomposition into a falsifiable
   claim.
2. **Fold occurrence-time stratification into the H0014 retry**
   (full-2026, ≥ 2027-01-15, already mandated by H0014's outcome B):
   pre-register the late-max fraction as an additional operationalized
   diagnostic, so a recurrence of the shift is attributable in-protocol
   rather than post-hoc.
3. **H0016 candidate: the H0011 mechanism question is now tractable.**
   H0011's amendment said mechanism identification "would require new
   occurrence-time data (e.g. parsed from raw CLI product text)" —
   E0001 demonstrates that parsing works (≥96% coverage). A
   pre-registered study of tmin occurrence times vs the local-midnight
   boundary can finally test the physical midnight-boundary claim
   H0011 could not.
4. **Platform work (small):** productionize the occurrence-time parser
   (currently exploratory-only in `scripts/e0001_chi_mechanism_exploration.py`)
   as a validated pipeline feature before any of the above pre-register
   against it, including handling of the shared ~3.5% unparsed-2026
   residue (F10).

## 6. Limitations

- One partial year, one shifted station; every 2026 monthly cell is
  n ≈ 28–31.
- Occurrence times come from the *final* product only; intermediate
  products were not parsed.
- The late-cutoff threshold (16:00) matches the products' stated
  "AS OF 0400 PM" but preliminary preparation may lag/lead by minutes;
  no sub-hour sensitivity analysis was done.
- ~3.5% of 2026 final products did not parse (F10); those days are
  excluded from occurrence denominators only.
- No forecast-side avenue was investigable: the forecast archive only
  begins 2026-07-20 (ADR 0003 — no historical backfill exists), so
  forecast issuance timing/horizon/update-frequency questions are out
  of reach for the 2023–2026 comparison and are, in any case, not part
  of the observation-side revision pipeline.
- Weather-regime characterization is deliberately shallow (volatility,
  heat-day counts); a meteorological attribution of *why* Chicago's
  2026 maxima ran late (frontal timing, cloud cover, wind regime) is
  beyond this archive's variables.
