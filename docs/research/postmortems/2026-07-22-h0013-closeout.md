## 6. Limitations

1. **Station-, not era-generalization.** CHI and NYC share the 2023–2026
   window and continental weather regimes; the samples are independent
   in station identity, not in time. Nothing here tests whether the
   effect persists into future eras.
2. **The 2026 partial-year gap collapse (registered descriptive
   observation, flagged for follow-up).** Per-year CHI rates are stable
   for tmin (25–26% every full year) and for tmax through 2025 (13.8%,
   13.9%, 14.1%) — but the 2026 partial year (Jan–Jul, n = 198) shows
   tmax at 50/198 = 25.25%, *exactly equal* to tmin's 50/198: the
   asymmetry is absent in the 2026 slice at CHI. Possible explanations
   (seasonal composition of a Jan–Jul window vs a full year; an NWS
   reporting-practice change in 2026) cannot be distinguished here, and
   NYC's per-year split was not registered and therefore not computed.
   These are point estimates without CIs, on a registered descriptive
   quantity — they cannot alter the pooled pre-registered outcome, but
   they materially qualify how current the confirmed asymmetry is. **A
   follow-up hypothesis (per RESEARCH.md step 5: an exploratory
   observation becomes a new entry, never a silent edit) should test
   whether the tmin/tmax revision asymmetry has weakened or vanished in
   2026 at both stations before any downstream work relies on its
   present-day magnitude.**
3. **Part 2 remains cadence-bound.** Both stations publish ≈2 issuances
   per variable-day, so post-midnight formation is near-universal for
   *both* variables (tmax 99.0% here). Part 2 adds label-formation
   facts, not mechanism evidence — the inherited scope statement is
   binding, and no physical midnight-boundary mechanism has been
   identified by any experiment to date.
4. **Matched-cadence scope.** CHI was chosen (pre-freeze, disclosed)
   because its cadence matches NYC's. The replication therefore
   generalizes the claim to matched-cadence stations only; DEN (3.86
   issuances/day) and LAX (2.35) are different regimes requiring their
   own pre-registered study.
5. **Same source pipeline.** Both archives come from the same IEM CLI
   backfill pipeline; a systematic archive-level artifact common to both
   stations would replicate spuriously. Mitigated (not eliminated) by
   H0012's independent validation of labels against Kalshi's actual
   settlement payouts (791/791) on the NYC side.

## 7. Recommendation — does the hypothesis generalize beyond NYC?

**Yes, with named scope limits.** The revision-rate asymmetry (Part 1)
is now demonstrated at two stations with near-identical effect sizes
(+11.0pp and +10.1pp, overlapping CIs) — at matched-cadence stations
over 2023–2026, the asymmetry is a property of the CLI settlement-label
formation process, not an NYC idiosyncrasy. Downstream consumers
(H0005/H0006 successors, any KXLOWT-family market work) should treat
variable-specific label noise as the norm: daily-LOW labels carry
roughly 1.6–1.8× the revision risk of daily-HIGH labels, and no label of
either variable should be treated as final before the post-midnight
report.

Two things are required before extending further:

1. **Recency check (new hypothesis):** test the 2026 gap-collapse
   observation (§6.2) at CHI and NYC before relying on the asymmetry's
   current magnitude.
2. **Cadence-regime replication (new hypothesis):** DEN/LAX, whose
   different issuance cadences also make them the natural testbed for
   whether Part 2's formation-timing claim is anything more than
   cadence.

The physical midnight-boundary *mechanism* remains unidentified and
untested (no occurrence-time data exists in the archive); that scope
limit is unchanged from H0011.
