## Scientific outcome

**Demonstrated.** (1) NYC tmin settlement labels revise materially more
often than tmax labels: 24.26% vs 13.26% of variable-days, an ~11.0-point
excess, Newcombe 95% CI [+8.00, +13.98] — now carrying a confidence
interval rather than H0002's exploratory point estimates, though computed
on the same archive that motivated the claim (a formalization, never to be
cited as independent replication). (2) Revised daily-minimum labels
essentially always (311/311) reach their final value only in a
post-midnight issuance — **settlement-label formation timing**.

**Not demonstrated.** The timing of the underlying physical temperature
event. The mandatory adjacent disclosure shows revised tmax labels are
*equally* post-midnight-formed (170/170), and 1,261/1,282 days carry the
two-issuance {~16:30 preliminary, ~01:30 final} cadence — E2's pass
reflects the CLI reporting cycle, exactly as the pre-registration's R1
risk predicted and the audit ruled. The physical midnight-boundary
mechanism remains unidentified; identifying it would require
occurrence-time data this frame does not carry, as a separate future
hypothesis (per the frozen scope statement — recorded, not proposed,
here).

**Practical consequence for downstream work.** Label-noise handling must
be variable-specific: daily-LOW (KXLOWT-family) labels carry roughly
double the revision risk of daily-HIGH labels, and no label of either
variable should be treated as final before the post-midnight report. This
sharpens, with a CI, the caution H0005's exploratory KXLOWTNYC read and
H0012's per-variable splits already suggested. NYC-only, 2023-2026;
generality awaits a second station.

## Lessons learned (from a clean run, deliberately recorded)

- **The hardened workflow held end-to-end on its first post-H0005
  outing.** Zero execution-time defects, zero superseded artifacts,
  byte-identity on the first rerun. The expensive H0005 lessons —
  exact-arithmetic decisions, frozen serialization, hand-derived
  formula cross-checks at matching precision, re-deriving from the frozen
  text rather than the code — were applied from the start here, and the
  integrity review's remaining findings were all protective (gate
  hardening, ordering fidelity) rather than outcome-relevant.
- **Pinning the dataset hash at pre-registration time** (possible because
  the frame already existed) is strictly stronger than execution-time
  pinning and cost nothing. Default to it whenever the data already
  exists.
- **Disclosing a known confound *and* pre-registering its measurement
  paid off.** R1 predicted E2 would saturate at ~1.0 for both variables;
  it did, exactly. Because the tmax attribution rate and gap
  decomposition were mandatory adjacent outputs, the published record
  self-limits — no reader can quote Part 2 without seeing the cadence
  baseline beside it. Pre-registering the *disclosure* of a weakness is
  as valuable as pre-registering the estimand.
- **The audit-question pattern worked.** The pre-registration explicitly
  asked the audit to rule on E2's identifiability rather than resolving
  it unilaterally; the audit's option-(b) ruling (proceed + binding scope
  statement) produced a Confirmed result that is honest by construction.
- **Fully deterministic designs are cheap insurance.** With no bootstrap,
  byte-identity is trivially achievable and every H0005-class
  float/ordering failure mode is structurally absent. Where a hypothesis
  doesn't need resampling, don't add it.
- **Provenance nuance for the record:** the manifest's
  `analysis_git_commit` (`425fbc9…`) predates the commit containing the
  analysis script itself; the closing commit (this closeout) carries the
  script, tests, protocol documents, and artifacts — the same
  "added in the commit containing this record" convention
  EXP-20260721-H0002 established.
